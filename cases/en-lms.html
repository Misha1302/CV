TuringSquare-LMS

Administrator, teacher, curator and student roles, course entitlements, payments and automated judging must keep their invariants across retries, network failures, migrations, and several judge workers running concurrently.

The project code is private. This case study intentionally excludes secrets, personal data, and operational identifiers.
01

Problem

Administrator, teacher, curator and student roles, course entitlements, payments and automated judging must keep their invariants across retries, network failures, migrations, and several judge workers running concurrently.

02

Constraints

Roles and course entitlements, duplicate orders and provider-side verification; a payment intent alone grants nothing. Plus judge isolation, a lease protocol, and migrations under live load.

03

Solution

Explicit roles and states, a balance ledger, verified webhooks, idempotent payment completion and critical-flow gates; an isolated judge with four parallel workers, a lease protocol, and a reproducible release/recovery process.

04

Outcome

The platform is in production and holds production load: 150+ students (product-owner data, 22 Nov 2026), a five-problem tour with 514 real tests and automated C++17/Python3 judging, and a judge running four parallel workers. Load runs on an isolated rig: 959 sequential requests with zero errors (p95 52–78 ms) and 2 880 synchronized requests with zero 5xx.

05

Verification

Live production acceptance: five-problem tour, 514 real tests, real C++17/Python3 verdicts read back after re-login, 128 confirmed student bindings, four-worker judge, isolated load measurements with an explicit NO_GO for unmeasured 200-user capacity.

06

My ownership

Architecture and acceptance criteria, agent-produced implementation under my review, the judge pipeline, release/recovery procedures, and the actual production acceptance.
