Projects as engineering cases

.NET backend · production LMS · judge · roles · entitlements

TuringSquare-LMS

Administrator, teacher, curator and student roles, course entitlements, payments and automated judging must keep their invariants across retries, network failures, migrations, and several judge workers running concurrently.

Architecture case study ↗
  • ProblemAdministrator, teacher, curator and student roles, course entitlements, payments and automated judging must keep their invariants across retries, network failures, migrations, and several judge workers running concurrently.
  • SolutionExplicit roles and states, a balance ledger, verified webhooks, idempotent payment completion and critical-flow gates; an isolated judge with four parallel workers, a lease protocol, and a reproducible release/recovery process.
  • ResultThe platform is live in production: 120+ active students as recorded in the repository, a five-problem tour with 514 real tests and automated C++17/Python3 judging, and a judge running four parallel workers. Load runs on an isolated rig: 959 sequential requests with zero errors (p95 52–78 ms) and 2 880 synchronized requests with zero 5xx, where 223 responses were the expected deterministic 409 on conflicting duplicates rather than failures.
.NET backend · payments · subscriptions · recovery

VpnMediator

Problem: Payment, subscription, and actual access can diverge after duplicate webhooks, timeouts, or process restarts.

Solution: Explicit access and payment states, idempotent operations, audit trails, background reconciliation, migrations, and verifiable recovery paths.

Result: Verifiable state transitions, backup/restore, health gates, and rollback-aware deployment; the public case study excludes secrets and user data.

Architecture case study ↗
Python · web/Android · offline-first · reliable synchronization

botay! / student-execution-os

Problem: Imported evidence, local edits, and LLM proposals can conflict; disconnected clients must not lose or silently overwrite user intent.

Solution: Separated external observations, canonical state, and derived plans; client operation IDs and atomic server records make offline replay idempotent. Feasibility requires validated witnesses and preserves UNKNOWN where proof is missing.

Result: Browser and native Android clients, migrations and device/release checks; the signed Android 0.8.3 release workflow passed on 10 Oct 2026. External adoption has not been established.
